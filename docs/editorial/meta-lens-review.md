# Six-dimension semantic review

Date: 2026-07-29

Ticket: KAN-263

Decision: approved with additive-use caveat

## Naming and order

The canonical order remains:

1. **Measure** - geometric, survey and quantitative relations.
2. **Witness** - evidential grounding, inheritance and uncertainty.
3. **Use** - fitness for navigation, retrieval, access and action.
4. **Cosmos** - meaning, orientation, naming, craft and staged knowledge.
5. **Power** - authority, property, jurisdiction, boundaries and institutions.
6. **Silence** - omission, erasure, refusal and what a system cannot record.

The sequence moves from formal relation through evidence and use to meaning,
authority and absence. It is a reading order, not a ranking.

`Witness` remains preferable to “Evidence” because it can describe copied,
partial and situated testimony without treating a source as transparent.
`Cosmos` remains preferable to “Symbolism” because the dimension also holds
orientation, classification and systems of ordered meaning. `Silence` remains
active rather than “Completeness” because absence may be produced or protected,
not merely deficient.

## Consistency decisions

- A native axis may map to several dimensions with weights between −1 and 1.
  Weights express crosswalk contribution, not truth or quality.
- The only inverse mapping remains Completeness → Silence. Higher native
  completeness reduces the corresponding silence contribution.
- Native labels remain visible beside every essay radar.
- The six normalized values support discovery and comparison only. They do not
  assert that a score of 0.8 in one essay is a measured quantity equivalent to
  0.8 in another.
- Same-label axes share a mapping only after contextual review. Near-synonyms
  are not collapsed.
- Canonical terms used by the starter example map directly to themselves.

## UI wording

The essay-level panel is labelled “Additive meta-lens,” not “unified score.”
Its caption states that values are editorial, normalized and
non-commensurable without the native crosswalk. The mapping disclosure precedes
any cross-essay radar work and preserves negative contribution wording as
“inverse.”

This review closes the semantic gate for KAN-10 while leaving the optional
cross-essay comparison in KAN-51 independently reversible.
