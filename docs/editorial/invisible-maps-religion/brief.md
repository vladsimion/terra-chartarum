# Invisible Maps of Religion — concept brief

**Ticket:** KAN-122  
**Primary room:** The Theatre  
**Secondary rooms:** The Road, The City

## Against-the-grain thesis

Religious maps do not merely illustrate belief. A sacred centre, eastward
orientation, pilgrimage sequence or liturgical city plan asks bodies to inhabit
space in a particular order. These images make cosmology operational: Jerusalem can
become a centre even for a reader who will never reach it, while an itinerary turns
devotion into a chain of expected stations.

The essay will resist the idea that a Jerusalem-centred map is simply geographically
“wrong.” Its question is what the map lets a community rehearse: origins, ends,
belonging, distance, danger and the hierarchy of places.

## Why Terra Chartarum

The site can set three spatial grammars beside one another:

- centred world pictures, where theological meaning structures the whole;
- itineraries, where sequence matters more than uniform scale;
- sacred city images, where ritual memory makes an urban plan.

The Invisible Maps series connects this essay to Trade: one reveals the institutions
beneath a route, the other the devotional orientation beneath a world picture.
Theatre is primary because the maps stage a cosmos and a reader’s place within it.
Road and City remain essential secondary rooms.

## Editorial contract

The corpus must span at least eight rights-cleared objects and include all three
grammars. The essay must distinguish Christian examples from “religion” in general;
this first corpus is a bounded medieval-European and Byzantine case study, not a
universal typology. Later expansion should add Jewish, Islamic, Buddhist, Hindu and
other mapping traditions only with equivalent language expertise and source depth.

The hero is the Hereford Mappa Mundi. Its density supports guided inspection, but
the argument must keep the smaller Psalter, Madaba mosaic and Matthew Paris itinerary
from becoming mere precursors.
