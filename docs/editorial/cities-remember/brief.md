# Cities Remember — concept brief

**Ticket:** KAN-130

## Thesis

A city map never records only the city that exists. It preserves demolished walls,
monumental ambitions, property regimes and obsolete risks, allowing past urban
orders to remain actionable long after the streets have changed.

## Room assignment

- **Primary:** The City
- **Secondary:** The Archive, The Theatre

The City is the subject and anchor. The Archive supplies survival, fragment and
revision; The Theatre explains how bird’s-eye views and city atlases stage civic
identity.

## Why this site

Terra Chartarum can do more than place an old map beside a new one. A
georeferenced, opacity-controlled Nolli overlay can expose continuity, demolition
and registration error in the same interaction. Catalogue records then connect the
overlay to fragments, bird’s-eye portraits, atlas views and insurance sheets.

## Boundary

The essay studies the city as selectively remembered by maps, not a complete history
of urban memory. It will distinguish a map’s original administrative, commercial or
representational purpose from the later evidence historians extract from it.
Georeferencing residuals remain visible; the overlay will not pretend that an early
plan and a modern basemap share perfect geometry.
