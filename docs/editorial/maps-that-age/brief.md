# Maps That Age - concept brief

**Ticket:** KAN-113  
**Primary room:** The Archive  
**Secondary rooms:** The Map, The Theatre

## Against-the-grain thesis

Maps do not simply become old. A copperplate can persist through decades of repair,
re-engraving, erased borders, renamed cities and new publishers. Later impressions
keep enough inherited geometry to look continuous even when the political argument
has changed. “Age” is therefore not only the interval since a map was printed; it is
an editorial history stored in the plate, paper and catalogue record.

The essay will read successive states against the habit of treating one title and
one nominal date as one stable object. Its central claim is that continuity is made:
revision lets a map retain authority by disguising change as maintenance.

## Why Terra Chartarum

The project already has the necessary grammar:

- catalogue records can identify edition, state, engraver, medium and related maps;
- `CompareSlider` can align successive impressions at the moment of revision;
- room metadata can separate the map’s measured surface from its archival life;
- Cartometry can export a reviewable state-change ledger instead of burying
  observations in prose.

The interactive should never imply pixel-perfect registration where scans have
different crops or distortions. It will name each observed change, its evidence and
its confidence.

## Editorial contract

The corpus must include at least eight rights-cleared maps and at least two defensible
same-plate or same-family comparisons. One comparison becomes the hero. Research must
distinguish physical plate wear, intentional revision, reissue and later colouring.
The essay will be publishable only when every visible claim about a state change maps
to a catalogue field and citation.

## Intended reader movement

1. Meet a map that appears stable.
2. Drag across a later state and notice one named alteration.
3. Learn how plates, publishers and catalogues produce continuity.
4. Inspect a structured ledger of the detected changes.
5. Return to the collection able to ask “which state?” before trusting “which map?”
