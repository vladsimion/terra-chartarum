# Invisible Maps of Trade - concept brief

**Tickets:** KAN-104 · **Primary room:** The Road · **Secondary rooms:** The Border,
The City

## Thesis

A portolan chart did not merely picture Mediterranean trade. By reducing land to a
named coast and sea to a routable field, it made a commercial network appear natural
while concealing the jurisdictions, labour, coercion and negotiated privileges that
kept that network moving.

The essay therefore reads the chart against the grain. Its rhumb network is not
treated as a neutral diagram of geographic knowledge but as the visible surface of a
political machine. Under every harbour name sits a differently constituted authority:
metropole, colony, quarter, tributary, feudatory, subject port or foreign fondaco.
Under every clean line sits a timetable, a hull, a crew, a customs arrangement and the
possibility of force.

## Why this belongs in Terra Chartarum

The portal can place historical sheets beside its own VMN authority tables, route
paths and commodity vocabulary. A reader can move from the beautiful abstraction of
the Carta Pisana to the dated political record behind a single call, then return to
the network and see that its apparent continuity was repeatedly remade. That
map–data–essay circuit is the argument; it could not be reproduced by a conventional
illustrated article.

## Room assignment

The Road is primary because the essay asks how repeated movement becomes a form of
space. The Border is secondary because each route crosses jurisdictions whose
differences the portolan suppresses. The City is secondary because Venice, Alexandria,
Constantinople and Candia work as legal and commercial devices as much as locations.

## Scope boundary

The essay uses the currently published VMN data and its source log. It does not claim
the page-level Lane/O’Connell chronology verification still reserved for KAN-154, nor
does it present the schematic route paths as reconstructed sailing tracks.
