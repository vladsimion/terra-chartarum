# Invisible Maps of Trade - research dossier

**Ticket:** KAN-106

## Argument ledger

1. **The portolan is a selective operating surface.** Its density lies at the coast:
   place-names, bearings and repeated coastal profiles. This is not deficient
   chorography. It is a successful answer to a maritime user’s questions.
   **Source:** Campbell 1987.
2. **The network was politically heterogeneous.** Venetian maritime power joined
   directly governed territory to quarters, privileges, tributaries, feudatories and
   foreign ports. Treating every node as a “colony” would erase the negotiations that
   made movement possible. **Sources:** Lane 1973; O’Connell 2009; VMN authority
   tables.
3. **A route was an institution, not a line.** Convoy organisation, shipbuilding,
   operating windows and ordered calls turned geographic possibility into repeatable
   commerce. **Sources:** Lane 1934; Lane 1973.
4. **The visual network suppresses its maintenance.** Labour, warehousing, customs,
   diplomacy and coercion are largely absent from the sheet even though they produce
   the movement it depicts. **Source:** Harley 2001.
5. **The digital Atlas must preserve uncertainty.** VMN route geometries are authored
   schematic corridors; authority rows are dated assertions with source keys. The
   essay may expose those models but must not describe them as recovered GPS tracks or
   final page-verified chronology. **Source:** Terra Chartarum VMN source log.

## Chronology used in the draft

The draft is anchored to broad, uncontroversial phases rather than disputed day-level
events: the emergence of surviving portolans around 1300; Vesconte’s signed chart of
1311; Andrea Bianco’s Venetian atlas of 1436; Fra Mauro’s world map of 1459; Roselli’s
chart of 1466; de’ Barbari’s Venice of 1500; and Agnese’s sixteenth-century atelier.
Political examples are read from the published authority table and described as model
rows, not as newly verified archival claims.

## Bibliography

The structured bibliography and claims-to-citations links live in the editorial
manifest. Core entries are Campbell’s open History of Cartography chapter, Lane’s
maritime history and shipbuilding study, O’Connell’s account of negotiated empire,
Harley’s critical-cartography essays, and the versioned VMN authority tables.

## Editorial cautions

- “Venetian” describes the network being analysed, not the maker of every sheet.
- `foreign_port`, `quarter`, `tributary`, `feudatory`, `subject` and `colony` retain
  distinct meanings.
- Commodity tags indicate documented route associations in the project dataset; they
  are not tonnage estimates.
- The essay does not infer wind, current or travel-time costs from modern reanalysis.
