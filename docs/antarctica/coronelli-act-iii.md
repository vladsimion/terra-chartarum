# Coronelli at the edge of the known world (KAN-422)

Act III's research package: what has been established, what has not, and why the
act cannot yet be written.

Tables: `data/antarctica/coronelli-lineage.csv`, `coronelli-annotations.csv`,
plus the Coronelli rows in `sources.csv` and `map-objects.csv`.

## What is established

Very little, and the package says so rather than filling the space.

Coronelli published the _Epitome Cosmografica_ in 1693, with a Cologne imprint
that is generally understood to stand for Venice, at the request of Andrea
Poletti. A digitised copy was located. The _Atlante Veneto_ appeared between
1690 and 1701 in thirteen folio volumes, conceived as a continuation of the Blaeu
_Atlas Maior_. A David Rumsey record exists for a Coronelli terrestrial globe of
about 1688.

That is the whole of it. Three works and one lead.

## What is not established

The plate. `Terre Polari, Artiche, ed Antartiche` is the object on which the
entire act rests, and it has not been seen: the Rumsey detail page returned an
access check during the audit and its metadata could not be transcribed.

Nothing in the Epitome has been transcribed either. There is no page locator for
the polar text, which means the ticket's requirement to read the graphic and
textual evidence together is not met and cannot be met from here.

Milanesi's monograph on Coronelli has not been consulted. Without it the act
would be reading plates as if they were opinions, which is the failure the claim
ledger flags as high risk for exactly this material.

No Atlante Veneto volume or plate has been identified as carrying polar material.
The object register holds a placeholder, and a placeholder that gestures at a
thirteen-volume work must resolve to named plates or be deleted.

## The finding that may be waiting

Search evidence during the audit suggested the 1693 polar plate is largely
occupied by the northern polar regions, annotated with recent voyages, and that a
separate `Terre Artiche` plate exists.

If that holds, the act inverts, and improves. The interesting thing about a
comprehensive late-seventeenth-century cosmographer is not what he invented in
the far south but how little he had to say about it, next to a north where there
were names, dates and tracks to engrave. A cosmographer with a globe to finish
cannot leave a hole in the southern calotte, and what he put there under that
constraint is a better question than what he believed.

This is recorded as a lineage row and a gap rather than written up, because it
rests on a search result and not on a plate.

## The annotation plan

Five steps, all with unresolved regions: the plate entire, the north, the south,
the globe's southern calotte, and the text the plate accompanies. No crop is
specified, because a crop of an unseen plate is a guess about where to point.

Four are `planned` and the fifth, the text reference, is `blocked`. The
validator refuses any annotation marked `built` while its region is unresolved.

## What would unblock it

In order: retrieve the Rumsey record or find a second digitised copy of the
plate; establish whether the Epitome carries a distinct southern polar plate;
acquire Milanesi; work through a digitised Atlante Veneto and list the plates
showing the southern polar region.
