# Coronelli at the edge of the known world (KAN-422)

Act III's research package: what has been established, what has not, and what the
act argues.

Tables: `data/antarctica/coronelli-lineage.csv`, `coronelli-annotations.csv`,
plus the Coronelli rows in `sources.csv` and `map-objects.csv`.

## The finding

Coronelli's polar section in _Atlante Veneto_ Tomo I carries two engraved plates,
`Terre Artiche` and `Polo Artiche`. Both are Arctic. No southern polar plate was
cut.

The obvious reading is that he had little to say about the far south. That
reading is wrong, and the text says so. The essay runs seven printed pages, pages
70 to 76, and the last of them is entirely southern: Nuyts in 1627, Tasman's
Terra di Diemens in 1642, New Zealand, Quiros's Terra Australe dello Spirito
Santo of 1606, Carpentaria. The south is attended to at length.

What that page mostly does is throw things out. Coronelli recounts the Sevarambi
at length and then rules the story suspect, because no author is named and its
circumstances are paradoxical. The Isle of Pines is `per favolosa pure viene
tenuta`. Frisquemore was invented in 1650 by two St Malo captains and divided
into twelve provinces on paper, with the account printed at Paris in 1662. The
Terra de' Papagalli goes the same way. Then he generalises, and names the rest:
the Strait of Anian, Frisland, Nova Albione, the Isle of St Borondon, Fonseca.
His reason is a good one. After their supposed discoveries, nobody was ever able
to find or see them again.

So the missing plate is a judgement, not a gap in attention. A cosmographer
cannot engrave a sheet of lands he has just finished calling fables. The north
got two plates because Willoughby and Martens could be named, dated and placed at
a latitude; the south got none because the claims that would have filled it were
the ones Coronelli had rejected.

That inference, from `he called these lands fables` to `therefore he cut no
plate`, is ours and not his. Milanesi is the control on it.

## What is established

**The plate.** `Terre Artiche`, David Rumsey list no. 12186.101, in _Atlante
Veneto_ Tomo I. Atlas map, 46 x 61 cm, scale 1:12,200,000, examined at full IIIF
resolution. Arctic to the neatline. Its dedication cartouche names Coronelli as
Cosmografo of the Republic and dedicates the sheet to Conte Ercole Pepoli,
Senator of Bologna: a commercial object with a patron, not a private cosmology.

**The plate's legend.** At the pole, where the inherited cartography drew four
islands around a central rock, Coronelli engraves a ring of mountains enclosing a
block of text, and that text is a voyage register: Hugh Willoughby at 76 degrees;
a party of seven sent to overwinter at Spitsbergen to test the China passage,
which sailed 30 August 1633 and returned `senz'alcun profitto` on 26 May 1634;
and Friedrich Martens of Hamburg, who left the Elbe on 15 April 1671 and returned
21 August having reached 81 degrees.

**The section.** Rumsey 12186.100 to .107, printed pages 70 to 76: a two-column
essay with marginal headings, closing into `Idrografia` at .108.

**Coronelli on his own inheritance.** At page 70 he describes the four polar
islands and the central rock, reports that their interiors are left blank, and
says it cannot be told whether this was sketched from life and actually seen or
`finto dall'imaginatione`, naming Mercator and the year 1570 among those who drew
it. This is the inherited-versus-observed distinction made in his own voice.

**Plate and text on one source.** Martens's 1671 voyage appears in the plate's
legend, in the essay at page 73 where it is tied to the whale fishery, and in the
source list closing page 76. The graphic and the textual evidence rest on a named
source in common.

**The Epitome is a different book with a different text.** _Epitome
Cosmografica_, Libro Secondo, Cap. IX, `Delle Terre Polari`, pages 265 to 266: a
two-page chapter, not the seven-page essay. It opens `Delle Terre Artiche, ed
Antartiche poco si puo dire, perche poco fin hora e giunto alla cognitione del
Mondo`, reports Magellan 1520, Le Maire 1616, Brouwer 1643, New Zealand 1654,
Nuits 1627 and Nuova Olanda 1644, dismisses the Terra de' Papagalli as `creduta
favolosa`, and gives the Arctic one paragraph ending in a Dutch claim to have
passed below the 82nd degree. Note that the plate says 81 for Martens and the
Epitome says 82 for the Dutch generally. They are different claims.

## What is not established

**Three of the seven leaves.** Pages 70, 73 and 76 have been read. Pages 71, 72
and 74 to 75, Rumsey 12186.102, .103 and .106, have not
(`ant-gap-atlante-polar-leaves`). The act's central argument currently rests on
three pages of seven, and says so.

**The rest of the Atlante Veneto.** Tomo I carries no southern polar plate; the
other twelve volumes and the _Libro dei Globi_ have not been audited
(`ant-gap-atlante-plates`).

**The globe.** No catalogued gore set has been opened and the southern calotte
has not been examined (`ant-gap-coronelli-gores`). A sheet map can decline to cut
a southern plate; a globe cannot leave a hole in the calotte, so what Coronelli
engraved there under that constraint is the sharpest remaining test of this
argument. Every lineage question against Mercator and Ortelius for the southern
coast stays blocked here.

**Milanesi.** Still unconsulted, and now carrying the specific question of what
Pepoli's patronage and the Frari workshop's practice mean for reading a
commercial plate as an argument (`ant-gap-coronelli-milanesi`).

## Corrections

The object register previously held `ant-obj-coronelli-terre-polari`, described
as a plate in the _Epitome Cosmografica_. Its identifier resolved to Rumsey
1012.002, a plate from the 1929 _Atlante internazionale del Touring Club
Italiano_. No Coronelli map plate of that title exists; the title belongs to text
leaves. Replaced by `ant-obj-coronelli-terre-artiche` and
`ant-obj-coronelli-polo-artiche`.

An earlier version of this act held that the south was what Coronelli did not
attend to. That was written before the text had been read, and it is withdrawn.

## The annotation plan

Five steps. Three carry specified IIIF regions on
`RUMSEY~8~1~303154~90060722` (full image 12365 x 7744): the plate entire, the
central polar legend at `5000,2350,2350,1200`, and the dedication cartouche at
`1620,650,1920,2160`.

Two keep unresolved regions: the globe's southern calotte, blocked on the gore
set, and printed page 76, the page that explains the missing plate, whose column
coordinates have not been fixed.

The plate is imaged in the bound volume and the gutter falls through the pole, so
the central legend sits on the fold. A DeepZoom target on the polar region has to
account for that or specify a different copy.

## A finding that belongs to another act

Page 76 sets out, in a named source with Coronelli's own verdict attached, a list
of invented lands: Frisquemore, the Isle of Pines, the Sevarambi, the Strait of
Anian, Frisland, Nova Albione, St Borondon, Fonseca, Terra de' Papagalli. That is
material for `ghost-geographies.csv` rather than for Act III, and it is recorded
here only so it is not lost.
