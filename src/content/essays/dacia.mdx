## Above the ground line they are read as inscriptions. Below it, they are dug as layers.

Nineteen centuries of maps of Dacia and Romania, staged twice. In the **Lapidarium**
each map stands as a carved stela and is read the way an epigrapher reads stone, with
the Six Readings. In the **Trench** the same maps lie at their proper depths and are
assessed in the field with the Five Seals. One year of history equals one millimetre
of earth; Ptolemy waits 1.87 metres down.

<Section id="brief" title="The church that is both" rooms={['border', 'theatre']}>

At Densuș, in Hațeg country, stands a small church built almost entirely of older
stones. Roman altars hold up its tower; a funerary inscription - a wife's farewell to
her husband, cut seventeen centuries ago - serves as a wall block. Walk around it and
you are in a museum; dig beneath it and you are in a trench. The lapidarium and the
excavation are the same building.

Maps of this land work the same way. Ptolemy's coordinates are reused by Renaissance
engravers; Austrian military sheets become the skeleton of the Romanian state map; a
name carved by a Greek geographer resurfaces, after eighteen centuries, on a city-limit
sign outside Cluj. Every map of Dacia is built from the _spolia_ of earlier maps - and
every map buries the one before it.

So this site has two wings. In the **Lapidarium**, each map is displayed as a carved
stela and read the way an epigrapher reads stone, with the Six Readings, adapted from
the Leiden conventions. In the **Trench**, the same maps are excavated as strata, each
at its proper depth, and assessed in the field with the Five Seals. Visit the museum
first. Then take the ladder down.

### Two instruments, one collection

**SEX·LECTIONES** - the Six Readings. Laboratory protocol, used in the museum.

| Sign | Reading        | What it asks                                                    |
| ---- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ‖    | **ORDINATIO**  | the geometric armature - projection, graticule, scale           |
| A·B  | **LITTERAE**   | letterforms and toponymy - who names the land, in what tongue   |
| [ ]  | **RESTITVTIO** | restored or inherited content - the copying chain               |
| ⟨ ⟩  | **CORRECTIO**  | instrumental correction - what was measured, verified, improved |
| ⟦ ⟧  | **RASVRA**     | erasure - what was struck out, censored, classified             |
| vac. | **VACAT**      | deliberate blank - terra incognita, voids, omission             |

**QVINQVE·SIGILLA** - the Five Seals. Field protocol, used in the trench.

| Sign | Seal           | What it asks                                                  |
| ---- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| M    | **MENSVRA**    | measurement - from symbolic space to geodetic survey          |
| A    | **AVCTORITAS** | authority - how much central power stands behind the map      |
| N    | **NOMINA**     | names - toponymic density and linguistic layering             |
| L    | **LIMES**      | boundaries - frontier zones, river lines, treaty demarcations |
| S    | **SILENTIVM**  | silence - what is withheld, by choice or by limit             |

Both score 0–5, in half-points. The marks record depths of a quality, not grades of
merit - an epigrapher's provisional verdicts: systematic, subjective, revisable.

### AVLA I · ANTIQVITAS · c. 150 - c. 350

Two Roman instruments, neither a picture in the modern sense: a table of coordinates
and a strip-diagram of roads. Between them they fix Dacia in number and in itinerary -
and both will be copied for a thousand years by hands that never saw the place.

recovered c. 1295, first printed with maps 1477. Klaudios Ptolemaios, Alexandria: a
coordinate catalogue, every drawing of it a later restoration.

Book III, chapter 8 of the _Geographia_ fixes Dacia between the Tisia, the Danube and
the Hierasus and then lists its contents: some forty-four towns with latitude and
longitude, fifteen tribes, and a royal seat - _Zarmizegethousa basileion_. Many of the
names carry the native _-dava_ ending, "settlement," in Greek dress: Piroboridava,
Ziridava, Sucidava. The land has been converted into a coordinate system.

The work survived as text. Recovered for the West around 1295 - Planudes may have
re-drawn the maps from the numbers alone - and printed with maps at Bologna in 1477, it
handed the Renaissance a Dacia that was pure data, waiting to be plotted. The first
stone in the wall is not a picture at all.

> **Find.** Dacia first becomes a set of numbers: every town a latitude, every error a
> coordinate.

_Nota._ Ptolemy's "map" of Dacia is a table. Every drawing of it - Byzantine,
Renaissance, modern - is a reconstruction from the numbers, which is why the numbers
outlived every sheet.

Nearly seven metres long and only thirty-four centimetres tall, the Peutinger roll
squeezes the known world into a ribbon. Geometry is sacrificed wholesale to connection:
it is a transit diagram, not a portrait. Across segments VII–VIII the Dacian roads climb
north of the Danube - Lederata to Tibiscum, on to Sarmategte, Apula, Napoca, Porolisso -
each station with its mileage, in the brisk Latin of a road-book.

Aurelian abandoned the province around 271–275; the roll's 4th-century content keeps the
roads anyway, and a scribe around 1200 copied them again, checking distances but never
shapes. The archive outlived the territory it described.

> **Find.** The map remembers what the empire forgot: Dacia's roads outlive Dacia by a
> century of parchment.

_Nota._ Pompeii (destroyed A.D. 79) and Constantinople (founded 330) share the same roll

- a palimpsest of centuries that copying flattened into one sheet.

On the great skin at Hereford the label DACIA sits in Scandinavia. This is not a blunder
but usage: by the thirteenth century chancery Latin used _Dacia_ for Denmark, and the
Dominican province of that name (1228) fixed the habit. The Carpathian–Danubian country,
meanwhile, is dressed in inherited ghosts - Moesia, Gothia, Scythia - with crusade-era
names crowding in from the documents: Cumania, _terra Blacorum_.

The map is ordered by theology, east at the top, Jerusalem at the centre. Authority and
inherited text supply everything; observation supplies nothing. The people of the land
persist in the charters even as the cartographers misplace its name by two thousand
kilometres.

> **Find.** The word DACIA migrates to Denmark; the land it named drops out of
> cartography's vocabulary.

_Nota._ The Dominicans' _Provincia Daciae_ (1228) covered Scandinavia - so in chancery
Latin "Dacia" meant Denmark for centuries, while papal files called the real country the
Diocese of Cumania (1227/28) and _terra Blacorum_.

### AVLA III · LITORA · THE COASTS · 1311

The sea is surveyed before the land. Genoese and Venetian pilots, working the Black Sea
after the Treaty of Nymphaeum (1261), draw the coast to a precision the interior will
not see for five centuries.

<h4 id="stela-vesconte">IV · PIETRO VESCONTE · PORTOLAN OF THE BLACK SEA</h4>

After the Treaty of Nymphaeum (1261) opened the straits, Genoese and Venetian pilots
worked the Black Sea hard - and their charts show it. Vesconte's coastline runs with
startling precision: the Danube's arms, then the ports written perpendicular to the
shore in red and black - _Licostomo_ (Chilia), _Moncastro_ (Cetatea Albă), _Vicina_
somewhere on the lower river, _Costanza_.

There is no graticule; the web of compass bearings is the geometry. Each crossing
corrected the last - iterative proofreading by keel. One league inland, the parchment
goes silent: the chart answers only the questions a pilot asks.

> **Find.** The sea taught Europe to measure before the land did.

_Nota._ Moncastro and Cetatea Albă are one fortress in two ledgers - Genoese and
Moldavian names for the same white castle on the Dniester liman. Vicina, richest port of
the lower Danube, is still missing: its exact site remains debated.

In Basel in 1532 a Saxon humanist of Kronstadt cut his homeland in wood: the first
separate printed map of Transylvania, made from inside it. The Carpathians stand as
molehill ranges around the rim; within the ring, towers and trees crowd a country he had
walked - hundreds of names, German foremost, with Hungarian and Romanian beneath.

This is chorography rather than geodesy - the land as a portrait, dense to the edges,
with hardly a blank to decorate. A decade later he folded the matter into his _Rudimenta
Cosmographica_ (Brașov, 1542), and the schoolbooks of half of Europe carried his
Transylvania for a century.

> **Find.** The first map of Transylvania drawn by someone who had slept in its towns.

_Nota._ Lazarus's _Tabula Hungariae_ (1528) showed the region first; Honterus gave
Transylvania its own sheet. A single print of 1532 is known to survive - and the claim
that he set south at the top is repeated more often than it is proven.

For the humanists, Dacia lived more vividly in Tacitus than in the Ottoman-vassal

old description, the present declined.

Rich occupation layer between the Carpathian scarp and the Dniester. Astonishing
find-density - some eight hundred named inclusions - but the spatial relationships drift,
as in material redeposited from memory rather than laid down in place.

_Field note._ Primary deposition occurred abroad; the layer reached this trench only in
1737, fourteen years after its maker's death. The richest pre-instrumental layer in the
entire section.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-cantacuzino">Stratum VI · 32.6 cm · Cantacuzino</h4>

Dense occupation layer south of the mountain line. The matrix is administrative -
tithe-rolls, county bounds, monastic foundations - deposited by the very offices that
governed the ground. Inclusions are bilingual in a peculiar way: Romanian content, Greek
casing.

_Field note._ Deposition was nearly a total loss. The layer survives as a few sealed
pockets and one reduced re-deposit of 1718; handle as unique.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-ortelius">Stratum VII · 43.1 cm · Ortelius</h4>

A strange, inverted layer: modern matrix, ancient inclusions. The frame and coastline date
the deposit to the late 16th century, but every artifact within it is 2nd-century
material, re-set with antiquarian care - spolia by design rather than by thrift.

_Field note._ The layer's most informative feature is what is missing. Contemporary
material was screened out before deposition; the excavator finds Trajan's bridge but not a
single Ottoman sherd. Erasure of the present, executed as scholarship.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-honterus">Stratum VIII · 49.4 cm · Honterus</h4>

First substantial layer above the sterile band: a true local deposit at last, after twelve
barren centuries. The matrix is dense and domestic - settlement debris evenly spread to the
mountain ring, trilingual inclusions throughout, German consistently uppermost in the
sorting.

_Field note._ The layer survives in a single fragile fragment. Its stratigraphic importance
is out of all proportion to its size: this is where the sequence restarts, and everything
above it is, in some measure, redeposited Honterus.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-vesconte">
  Stratum IX · 71.5 cm · Vesconte <em>(lens)</em>
</h4>

Second lens within the sterile band, water-laid: a bright, sharply sorted strip of coastal
material against the section's east face, fading to nothing within a hand's breadth of the
shoreline. The sorting is diagnostic - deposited by repeated tides of traffic, each voyage
reworking the last.

_Field note._ Where the Hereford lens is all matrix and no measurements, this one is all
measurement and no matrix. The two lenses together bracket the hiatus: faith without survey
above, survey without hinterland below.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-hereford">
  Stratum X · 72.6 cm · Hereford <em>(lens)</em>
</h4>

Thin lens within the sterile band - not a layer of the local sequence at all, but
wind-blown material from another deposit entirely. In section it reads as imported fill:
theological matrix, no measurable inclusions, the toponym DACIA found displaced far to the
north of its source.

_Field note._ Absence of local material is the find. Twelve centuries of this country
produced no surviving map of itself; what blew in instead was other people's memory of it.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-peutinger">Stratum XI · 167.6 cm · Peutingeriana</h4>

Thick, compressed layer directly over the Ptolemaic scatter. The deposit is linear:
road-gravel in section, stations at measured intervals, almost no material between routes.
Off the alignments the soil is clean - not erased, simply never deposited.

_Field note._ The layer is chronologically disturbed. Material from the 1st century
(Pompeii) lies beside material from the 4th (Constantinople); the medieval re-deposition
c. 1200 flattened three centuries into one horizon. Treat dates within the layer with
suspicion; trust the mileages.

</div>

<div data-scrolly-step>

<h4 id="stratum-ptolemy">Stratum XII · 187.6 cm · Ptolemaeus</h4>

Deepest cultural layer of the trench, immediately above sterile soil. The deposit is not a
drawn artifact but a dense scatter of coordinate pairs - the residue of itineraries, army
reports from Trajan's wars, and Marinus of Tyre's earlier compilation, audited once by an
Alexandrian and then by no one for a thousand years.

_Field note._ The find is in suspiciously good condition. Numbers do not fade the way
parchment does; the error pattern (longitudes stretched by caravan-time) is itself
diagnostic of the survey method.

</div>

### Site notes

Verdicts are interpretive, not metric: an epigrapher's readings, offered to be argued
with. Dates of medieval artifacts follow their content where the copy is later (the
Peutinger roll is dated here by its 4th-century matter, not its c. 1200 parchment). Claims
that resist verification - Honterus's orientation, Drobeta in Ptolemy's catalogue, the
exact site of Vicina - are marked uncertain rather than asserted.

This essay is a cousin of _Speculum Chartarum_ and its Six Bearings - same family,
different instruments. The Five Seals and Six Readings were cut for Dacia alone.

the next survey._
