# A name is not a thing. It is a job, and the same word can be given several.

Dacia is a Roman province, and then a different province on the other bank of
the river, and then a civil diocese that contains the second, and then a legend
struck on coinage, and then - for four centuries of Latin writers who had never
heard of the Carpathians - Denmark. The word does not change. Everything it
points at does.

<Section id="slice" title="One word, taken at its word" rooms={['border', 'archive']}>

This page is deliberately smaller than the trench it belongs to. It follows one
lexical form through the uses the corpus has actually cleared, and it exists to
prove that a single career can be traced end to end: from the witness that
attests it, through the compiled corpus, into the Atlas, and back out to a
citation a reader can check.

Nothing on this page is written twice. The periods, referents, fate classes,
locators and confidences below are the `name_uses` ledger of the Corpus Nominum
Daciae, compiled by the release build. Correcting a date in the corpus corrects
this page on the next build; demoting a use below review removes it from this
page on the same run. There is no second copy of the chronology to drift.

</Section>

<Section id="careers" title="The careers of the word" rooms={['border', 'archive', 'map']}>

Each step is one documented use: who used the name, what they meant by it, when,
on what witness, at what locator, and how far the editor is prepared to stand
behind it. The fate class names what the use did to the word - applied it,
carried it elsewhere, gave it back.

<NameCareers caption="Uses of the name Dacia cleared for publication. Compiled from the Corpus Nominum Daciae name-use ledger; unreviewed uses are counted in the ledger below rather than shown." />

The two uses on either side of 1300 are the reason the trench exists. A Roman
province on the Danube and a Latin name for Denmark share a string and nothing
else, and the corpus records that as an explicit non-relationship rather than
leaving a reader to infer descent from the spelling.

<Section id="path" title="What the slice proves, and what it does not" rooms={['archive', 'border']}>

Three careers open the Atlas on the ground their referent occupied, and the map
offers the way back to this passage. Three do not, and say so: Dacia Aureliana
and the civil diocese lie south of the Danube, where no layer has been compiled
yet, and the Scandinavian usage lies outside the extent of every Dacia layer
there is. That last one is the argument, not a gap in the software. The word
left the ground the map covers.

The map and flow figure do not manufacture a migration to make the sequence
look complete. Every reviewed use becomes a node and an inspectable Atlas state;
a relationship becomes a line only after a person clears its own ledger row.
The current ledger has ten Dacia relationship records and no reviewed
relationship, so no line is drawn. Chronology is visible, but chronology is not
continuity.

Nor is the trench's evidence complete: two further uses of the name are
compiled and withheld because nobody has cleared them, and no visual witness in
the ledger yet carries rights that permit publication.

</Section>
