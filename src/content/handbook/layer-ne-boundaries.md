## What you are looking at

Land borders as they stand today, from Natural Earth's small-scale public-domain
dataset.

This record is deliberately short. A context layer does not owe a reader a
reconstruction-and-uncertainty section, because it makes no historical claim to
be uncertain about. What it owes is its source, its licence, and a plain warning
about what it is doing on a historical map.

## How to read this layer

As a grid, and nothing else. Every line here postdates almost everything else on
this atlas. Switching it on over Roman Dacia, the stato da màr or the Hanseatic
corridors produces a picture in which modern states appear to have been present
at events that preceded them by centuries, and the resemblance is the reader's
own eye supplying continuity that the evidence does not.

The layer is machine-classified as `context` for this reason. The registry
schema refuses to let a context layer claim a historical category at all, so
these lines cannot be filed as territorial evidence even by accident.

## Sources and evidence

Natural Earth, `ne_110m_admin_0_boundary_lines_land`, public domain. Made with
Natural Earth. The registered temporal envelope begins at 1949, which is a
conservative floor for "the modern political map" rather than a claim that these
particular lines existed in that year.
