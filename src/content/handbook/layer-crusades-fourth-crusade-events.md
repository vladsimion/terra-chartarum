## What you are looking at

Three events: the diversion to Zara in November 1202, and the two assaults on
Constantinople in July 1203 and April 1204.

## How to read this layer

The position of each point is the _place_, not the event. A state record in this
dataset has no geometry of its own; it names the places it happened at, and the
layer draws it there. Every feature declares `modern_reference` so the borrowing
is visible.

That matters most for the diversion. What changed at Zara was the terms of a
contract, and the point on the map marks where the negotiation happened rather
than a thing that had an extent.

## Historical scope

1202 to 1204.

## Sources and evidence

Villehardouin was there and wrote as a participant with a case to make. Robert of
Clari was there and lower down the hierarchy, and disagrees. Niketas Choniates
was inside the city in 1204 and is the reason the sack has a victim's account in
this corpus rather than only the crusaders'.

All three are `research_only`, every locator reads `pending`, and this layer is
therefore not evidence of anything except what the project has compiled.

## Reconstruction and uncertainty

Dates are recorded to the month. The chronicles disagree on days, and settling
that here would settle it by data entry.

## Editorial decisions

The 1203 and 1204 assaults are two records, not one. They had different causes,
different outcomes, and only the second ended in the sack.
