## What you are looking at

The institutional history of the League as points in space and time: privileges
granted, confirmed and restricted; embargoes; conflicts and the peace treaties
that ended them; Hansetage; Kontor rules, relocations and closures; and the
institutional afterlives that outlasted the League itself.

## How to read this layer

This is where a network's history lives when it has no territory to have a
history in. The League's power was exercised by act - a privilege obtained, a
trade suspended, a diet convened - and each of those happened somewhere.

Most of these events are institutional rather than military. Privileges and
Hansetage outnumber conflicts, which is itself a finding about how the League
worked.

Symbol size follows `certainty`, so a firmly dated act reads larger than one
placed by inference.

## Historical scope

1295 to 1764. The end date is deliberate: it reaches past the last Hansetag into
the afterlife, because the institutions did not stop existing when the meetings
stopped.

## Sources and evidence

After UNESCO's documentation, Wubs-Mrozewicz, Henn, and Lambert and Sicking.
Each event carries its source key.

## Reconstruction and uncertainty

**Dating is the uncertainty here**, not location - these acts happened in named
cities. Where a privilege was negotiated over years and confirmed later, the
event takes the date of the act the sources emphasise, and `certainty` records
how firm that is.

Some events are attached to a city because that is where the instrument was
issued, which is not always where the thing it describes happened.

## Data fields

Event type and `certainty`; every feature carries its date and source keys. See
[the Hanseatic data fields](/atlas/handbook/data-fields/hanseatic/).
